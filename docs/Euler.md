# Project Euler on an Alchitry Cu

Ten Project Euler problems solved by HDLean designs that fit the
[Alchitry Cu](https://alchitry.com/boards/cu) (Lattice iCE40-HX8K: 7680 logic
cells, 32 block RAMs of 4 kbit, no DSP blocks). Each design aims at the shortest
time to the answer: few cycles *and* a high clock. They are in
[`Designs/Euler`](../Designs/Euler), one file per problem, each starting with a
description of its algorithm and hardware.

What is checked today, for every design:

* the solver's answer matches a plain-Lean reference implementation of the
  problem (simulated in Lean for the short runs, and always in Verilator on the
  emitted SystemVerilog, with the Lean and Verilator cycle counts compared when
  both run);
* the emitted SystemVerilog is read back by the proved parser before use;
* the whole board design (solver, cycle counter, UART reporter, PLL) places and
  routes with nextpnr, and meets timing at the PLL frequency reported below.

The designs carry no correctness proofs yet; the references and the
simulations are the evidence. The numbers come from the tools, not from a board
on the bench.

## The designs

**10. Summation of primes.** A segmented sieve of Eratosthenes over the odd
numbers below two million: fifteen engines in lockstep, each owning a block of
4096 odd numbers (one block RAM) and a block RAM of per-prime offsets. Primes
from 17 on strike one bit per cycle (a read–modify–write; consecutive strikes
land in different words), 3 … 13 whole words at a time, with a pattern shifted by the
offset. Three read-back units, each serving five engines, sum the primes with
additions only (count, running sum of counts, and positions via popcounts of
bit masks), clearing the words for the next segment. The 222 sieving primes and
their offsets are produced on chip first, by trial division with residues and
modular doublings.

**14. Longest Collatz sequence.** Only 333 333 starts can win (a start below
750 000 not divisible by 3, or below 500 000, lies on a larger start's chain).
Twenty-four lanes take two Collatz steps per cycle (`x = 4q + r` becomes `q`,
`3q + 1`, `3q + 2` or `9q + 8`, one addition of shifted copies) until the value
drops below 2048; the rest of the chain comes from a table built first, in four
block RAM copies, each serving six lanes through an arbiter and a pipeline that
adds the tail and keeps the copy's best.

**25. 1000-digit Fibonacci number.** The leading digits of the Fibonacci
numbers as an 18-digit BCD window, two terms per cycle, with a count of the
digits dropped when the window overflows. BCD additions resolve their decimal
carries with a single binary carry chain.

**31. Coin sums.** The coin-change dynamic programme as a systolic pipeline,
one stage per coin, amounts streaming through one per cycle; each stage adds its
own output from `c` amounts earlier, kept in a register or a block RAM delay
line. All eight coins in one pass: 209 cycles.

**43. Sub-string divisibility.** Ten lanes, one per last digit, search the
pandigital numbers from the right a digit per cycle, pruning on every
three-digit window: each level keeps the residue its window needs from the new
digit, so a candidate costs one table lookup and a compare; the digit registers
double as the search stack.

**48. Self powers.** By the Chinese remainder theorem, modulo `5^10` and `2^10`:
six lanes of left-to-right square-and-multiply on radix-4 interleaved modular
multipliers (multiplications by `n` use only its five radix-4 digits), terms
with `10 | n` skipped, the two residue sums combined once at the end.

**57. Square root convergents.** Exact numerators and denominators of up to 384
digits, as 32-digit BCD words in block RAM, streamed a word per cycle through a
pipeline (`q' = p + q`, then `p' = q' + q`, written back in place, then the
digit lengths, from the highest nonzero words).

**58. Spiral primes.** The diagonal values by additions only, a filter of
residues modulo the primes up to 61, then Miller–Rabin (bases 2, 7, 61,
deterministic far beyond the values involved) on five lanes with radix-4
carry-save Montgomery multipliers; jobs go to any idle lane and their verdicts return through a reorder
buffer to an in-order collector that tests the ratio layer by layer.

**92. Square digit chains.** Seven rounds of a streamed convolution count the
7-digit strings by digit-square sum (a delay line with ten taps and a pipelined
adder tree, ping-ponging between block RAMs), while a memoised chain engine in
BCD classifies the 567 possible sums; the last round adds up the counts of the
sums that reach 89.

**97. Large non-Mersenne prime.** By the Chinese remainder theorem: modulo `5^10`
square-and-multiply on a radix-4 interleaved multiplier, where multiplying by two
is a doubling with a conditional subtraction; modulo `2^10` the same steps on a
ten-bit register.

## Writing a solver

A solver's registers are a record: `hdl_record` fields with their reset values,
composed with `Both` and `Lanes` for the parallel parts (P14's 24 walkers and 4
table pipelines, P58's lanes and reorder buffer). The body reads the record as
handles and returns `done`, `answer` and the next record, usually as
`{ r with … }`; memories are read and written by name. When the builder rejects
a solver, `hdlean euler-sim`/`euler-emit` print why: the failing operation, its
widths and the enclosing scopes (each solver runs in `scope "P0NN"`, each lane in
`scope "lane i"`), for example (from `Tests/Euler.lean`)

```
toy > low half: zero extension from 8 to 4 bits (narrower: use a slice) (after 3 cells)
```

## On the board

[`boards/alchitry-cu/euler_top.v`](../boards/alchitry-cu/euler_top.v) is the
only hand-written logic: the PLL, a reset synchronizer, the solver, a cycle
counter on the solver's clock and the UART reporter on a 50 MHz clock of its own
(so that it never limits the solver). Once the solver raises `done`, the
reporter sends `<answer> <cycles>\r\n` over the board's USB serial port (115 200
baud, 8N1) about once a second, and the eight LEDs show the low byte of the
answer. The reporter and the counter are HDLean modules too
([`Designs/Euler/Report.lean`](../Designs/Euler/Report.lean)); a Lean test
simulates the reporter and decodes its serial line.

## Reproducing

```sh
lake build hdlean
python3 scripts/euler.py --eda /path/to/oss-cad-suite/bin            # all problems
python3 scripts/euler.py 31 --eda /path/to/oss-cad-suite/bin        # one problem
python3 scripts/euler.py 58 --no-lean-sim --eda ...                  # long runs: Verilator only
python3 scripts/euler_table.py                                      # the table above
```

For each problem the script simulates the solver in Lean and compares it with
the reference (`hdlean euler-sim`), emits the solver, counter and reporter
(`hdlean euler-emit`), runs the solver in Verilator, synthesizes the board design
with Yosys, finds the achievable clock with nextpnr, closes timing at the
fastest PLL frequency below it (`icepll`) and writes the bitstream
(`build/euler/pNNN/euler.bin`, for `iceprog`). Results go to
`build/euler/report.json`. The OSS CAD Suite build is the one pinned in
[Physical.md](Physical.md).
